# D2R character lighting: evidence and Crimson implementation

Updated 2026-10-16 for separate Character lighting, finite Local World omni illumination, contact-hardening point shadows, distance attenuation and a Key-driven player silhouette. The implemented-contract sections describe the current code. This is Crimson's artistic design, not a claim that D2R uses the same undocumented formula. Earlier punctual-rig measurements and build evidence are explicitly historical below; current integration results are recorded separately when available.

## Primary evidence

**Resurrecting a Classic**, physical PDF page 113, describes three directional readability lights for players and two for monsters/items. Artists independently control direction, color, intensity, diffuse response and specular response. Environment lighting is separate. Time of day and trigger-volume overrides are additional systems. [Developer presentation, page 113](https://media.gdcvault.com/GDC+2022/Speaker+Slides/Resurrecting+a+Classic_Todisco_Kevin.pdf#page=113).

Pages 133–137 describe global attenuation derived from punctual light influence and depth. Page 135 explicitly includes lighting rigs, IBL and emissive materials. This supports a broader darkness response than IBL alone, but does not establish the formula for shadows from unrelated lights. [Developer presentation, pages 133–137](https://media.gdcvault.com/GDC+2022/Speaker+Slides/Resurrecting+a+Classic_Todisco_Kevin.pdf#page=133).

**An Overview of the Diablo II: Resurrected Renderer**, physical page 18, lists a main directional shadow map, a separate character-shadow map, static-geometry shadow caching for spot lights and omnidirectional player-light shadows. The limited fixed-camera range avoids cascades. Pages 7–8 reject baked lighting; cached static depth is not baked illumination. [Developer presentation, page 18](https://media.gdcvault.com/GDC+2022/Speaker+Slides/An+Overview+of_Todisco_Kevin.pdf#page=18).

These are distinct contributions: actor readability, player-local illumination, world lighting/shadows and global attenuation. Crimson's two character lights, finite Local World omni and Key silhouette are a user-selected design inspired by those contributions.

## What the evidence cannot establish

Neither complete presentation specifies that a directional character map multiplies every torch's contribution, which player readability light produces the visible ground shadow, or the exact caster/receiver exclusions. Original Diablo II's skewed sprite shadows, discussed in the historical sprite section, are not evidence of D2R's shadow composition.

A screenshot of a crisp hero shadow next to a torch can show the torch's own character shadow. A dark pillar can still cast a shadow: receiving illumination and blocking a light are independent. Its visible faces may be back-facing, occluded, intentionally dark, or its shadow may fall outside the visible lit region. Do not infer universal pillar exclusion or a shared shadow overlay from the screenshot alone.

## Implemented Crimson contract

`SceneCharacter::lightRig` owns three jobs in **Character → Light Rig**. The master defaults disabled. **Character Lighting → Key / Fill** stores two world-space directional contributions with Color, Intensity and advanced Diffuse/Specular response. They illuminate only the designated Player Character subtree, ignore World occlusion and consume no ordinary scene-light slot. Directions do not rotate with character facing or parent scale.

**Local World Light** is an ordinary finite omni at presented player position plus world-up Height. Only World geometry receives and casts into its cube shadow; it does not illuminate the player or create a second player silhouette. Controls are Enabled, Height, Color, Intensity, Radius, Attenuation, Near Falloff Radius, Range Falloff, Edge Softness, Source Radius, Specular Scale, Cast Shadows and advanced biases. It shares the combined 4,096-source limit and sixteen-map/four-update budgets. Its transient source identity avoids authored light IDs without creating an ECS entity. The former World Fill response, shared lighting footprint and enlarged shadow range have been removed.

The default player point uses height 2 m, intensity `64/9`, radius 8 m, distance attenuation 2, near falloff 1.5 m, range exponent 1, edge softness 1, specular scale 0 and source radius 0. It uses normal Lambert/PBR response and real geometric occlusion. All ordinary point lights expose the same response controls. Their defaults remain attenuation 2, near 0, exponent 4, softness 1, specular 1 and source radius 0, preserving their prior appearance.

For source distance `d`, radius `R`, near radius `n`, range exponent `p`, edge softness `e` and distance attenuation `a`:

```text
t = clamp((d/R - (1-e))/e, 0, 1)
lightingFalloff = (1 - t^p)^2 * max(d*d + n*n, .01)^(-a/2)
```

Attenuation is the Godot-style distance exponent, bounded -10–10 and default2. Zero keeps distance energy constant before the range window, one gives inverse distance, and two preserves the former inverse-square division exactly. Negative values grow with distance. Near falloff and the existing .01 squared-distance floor remain Crimson safeguards; nondefault exponent evaluation is bounded to retain finite HDR. Range Falloff is bounded .25–8; Edge Softness .05–1. With exponent 1 and softness 1, the source fades continuously across the full range. Smaller softness concentrates the range cutoff into a smaller outer band. Near Falloff reduces the inverse-square peak. At and beyond Radius, contribution is zero. The ordinary 4/1 case retains its exact old quartic arithmetic without sqrt/pow.

**Specular Scale = 0** removes this source's GGX highlight without changing diffuse illumination or shadows. **Source Radius** controls the simulated emitter size for contact-hardening point shadows as well as the existing broadened specular lobe. Positive radius softens shadows with increasing blocker/receiver separation even at Specular Scale zero. Zero retains the original point-shadow filter. The highlight uses UE 5.8's isotropic soft-radius idea: angular size squared is `min(sourceRadius² / max(d², 1e-6), 1)` and GGX alpha squared increases by that value divided by `3.6*VoH + .4`, capped at one. The ordinary diffuse lobe and independent influence Radius/falloff remain unchanged. Crimson's combined control is an artistic source-size convention; it does not copy every part of either engine's spherical area-light integration.

The prior Crimson **Soft Source Radius** affected specular only. That followed UE's separate `SoftSourceRadius`, which broadens GGX but is not the emitter radius driving the inspected soft-shadow paths. UE's `SourceRadius` is distinct. See `Engine/Shaders/Private/CapsuleLightIntegrate.ush:62–99`, `ShadingModels.ush:129–187`, `VirtualShadowMaps/VirtualShadowMapProjectionSpot.ush:306` and `Source/Runtime/Engine/Classes/Components/PointLightComponent.h:40–52` in the supplied UE 5.8 checkout. Godot's **Size** instead affects shading and positional PCSS: its `scene_forward_lights_inc.glsl:520–606` searches blockers and expands the filter by source extent times `(receiverDistance-blockerDistance)/blockerDistance`. Its angular diffuse wrapping is separate and is not added to Crimson. The user clarified on 2026-10-16 that shadow softness was the intended effect.

Crimson's point PCSS uses the existing D32F comparison atlas. A bounded deterministic search takes 12 raw depth samples and decodes stored perspective face depth into radial blocker distance. The receiver triangle plane rejects self-depth and supplies corrected references for the 16-tap comparison filter. Its fixed plane-based disk orientation uses a canonical normal sign so opaque prepass, generated/fallback fragments and analytical reveal choose the same kernel without an animated sampling pattern. Every wide sample resolves its cube direction to the appropriate face; the filter cannot simply clamp a wide disk into two guard texels or cross into another light/Key/directional tile. No extra sampler, shadow map or shadow-render pass is required. Source Radius edits change sampling while reusing the six cached depth faces. The positive-radius query adds filtering work; zero takes the original 3×3 path. The dedicated Key silhouette and directional filters stay independent.

The wider filter exposed a prepass precision issue: encoding an exact axis normal into two unsigned 8-bit octahedral coordinates slightly tilts its decoded receiver plane. Transparent receivers retain derivative planes, so shadow-aware reveal could disagree at the penumbra. The current prepass packs signed 12-bit octahedral coordinates into the existing G/B/A bytes, preserving exact zero coordinates; R still stores the receiver scope. This improves plane precision without changing attachment memory. GPU regressions compare opaque compute and fallback/generated transparent queries on both axis-aligned and translated, rotated planes with the same image tolerance.

The Key independently owns **Cast Shadow**, Strength, Range and biases. Its normalized world direction controls both Character illumination and the player-only silhouette cast onto World receivers. It adds no World illumination and multiplies reflected lighting once, retaining a strong silhouette beside bright torches. Fill casts no shadow. Transient World/Character categories remain separate from authored direct/ambient masks.

**Character → Darkness Reveal → Spread** retains an independent analytic Gaussian reveal at half Character height. It emits no direct light and creates no point record or cube shadow. Selecting the player/subtree shows Key/Fill arrows, the matching Key Shadow arrow, actual Local World radius and a **50% Reveal** sphere in Scene view. Show Rig retains unselected/Game visualization. There is no standalone rig panel. Edit-mode controls support scene history; Play edits are temporary and Stop discards them.

Save writes `lightRig.version=4` with `localWorld`, Key/Fill and Key-shadow data. V2 punctual settings retain their original intensity/radius/near/specular and use exponent 4, softness 1 and source radius 0. Former v3 World Fill converts once: radius is `max(2*oldSpread, 1.25*height)`, capped to the valid finite point range, and intensity approximately matches its old foot irradiance through the new point response. Height/color/enabled/shadow biases remain authored. Old heights outside that finite radius cannot preserve foot illumination; the conversion no longer creates an enlarged lighting/shadow footprint. Save emits only current fields. Reader-only migration preserves existing project data without retaining a legacy renderer mode.

## Shared darkness composition

Let `D` be the Environment Ambient Darkness factor. All reflected light receives it, including ordinary directional/point lights, Local World Light, Character Key/Fill and diffuse/specular IBL. After each contribution's role/material/shadow response:

```text
reflected = (sceneDirect + localWorld + characterLighting + IBL) * D
reflected *= isWorld && receiveShadows ? keySilhouette : 1
surface = reflected + emission * (Affect Emission ? D : 1)
```

Player reveal remains `W=exp2(-q*q) * (1-smoothstep(6,8,q))`, where `q` is distance from the half-height player center divided by Spread. Spread is half visibility. The stable response is `baseline=1+(Floor-1)*Strength; Vp=baseline+(1-baseline)*W`. Scene-light energy produces `Vl` using Environment Threshold/Softness and available shadow maps; `D=max(Vp,Vl)`. Without enabled player reveal only `Vl` applies. Global darkness off, Floor one, Strength zero or zero ambient acceptance gives `D=1`.

The player distance reveal can cross walls; it is not room visibility or fog of war. Local World Light itself uses its own finite radius and shadow maps. Turning its Specular Scale to zero cannot remove an IBL highlight or another light's reflection; isolate those contributions when diagnosing blobs.

The point record contains six vec4 texels. Row 3 Y stores Source Radius; row 4 stores near radius squared, Specular Scale, exponent and edge softness. Row 5 X stores distance attenuation; reflected `u_clusterRecordLayoutV5` rejects older five-texel records. Generated identity is now v15, including the separate Environment Ambient Light uniform. Dependency content hashes invalidate affected shader programs when the PCSS include changes. Response-only edits upload records without rebuilding unchanged cluster culling. Writers emit only `sourceRadius`; reader-only migration consumes the earlier `softSourceRadius` name without changing its stored value.

## A character shadow disappearing near a torch

Before this separation, the inspected saved scene had two torches with Cast Shadows enabled but `shadowCasterMask = World`, excluding the Character. Their unoccluded additive light filled another light's player shadow. This was a lighting-role/composition issue, not evidence of corrupted material files or inadequate directional-map resolution. Authored torch masks were not silently rewritten.

The immediate existing control is **Torch → Advanced Lighting → Shadow Casters → Character**, alongside World if both categories should block the torch. Cast Shadows must also be enabled on the light and relevant model. Do not silently rewrite an authored World-only mask or force Character into every light. The Inspector tooltip now states that the mask selects blockers of that source and that Character is required for its player shadow.

In the former ordinary per-light composition, for a key contribution `K`, local torch `T` and ambient `A`, contrast from the key's shadow alone is approximately `K / (K+T+A)`. A dominant unoccluded torch naturally reduces it. A sharper or larger directional map cannot fix that contrast loss. Retaining contrast through the torch's own shadow requires character occlusion for that source; its shadow then follows the torch's geometry and can differ from the Key silhouette. The current artistic silhouette described below provides an independent way to retain player contrast.

Diagnose key-only, torch-only, then their sum. Check admitted point-map residency and shadow update counts, not only Cast Shadows. A stationary torch must invalidate its cached content when a character moves; a complete six-face update publishes coherently. The scheduler can defer caster-only work under load, so test the one-light case before attributing a dense-scene delay to filtering.

The user subsequently chose an explicit artistic Key silhouette that darkens World reflected lighting, including other lights and IBL, by its Strength. It does not transfer World/pillar shadow maps to unrelated lights, because only player geometry casts into this map. It adds no illumination to World, requires some reflected illumination to be visible, and leaves emission and fog independent. This preserves a controllable player silhouette beside bright torches. It is not claimed as D2R's undocumented formula.

## Authoring and acceptance

1. Designate the Player Character and enable its Light Rig in the Character Inspector. Use Scene view to rotate the editor camera while tuning; save edit-mode settings before Play.
2. With scene lights/IBL temporarily isolated, enable Local World Light and Cast Shadows. Move around a pillar and doorway. The finite source should be blocked by World geometry, retain pillar shadows and produce zero direct light outside Radius. An open door can physically pass light into the next room.
3. Start Range Falloff and Edge Softness at 1, Near Falloff at 1.5 m and Specular Scale at 0. Set Radius to the room scale; adjust Intensity afterward. Height controls shadow projection. These are point response controls, not a shared reveal footprint.
4. Keep Specular Scale at zero and compare Source Radius 0, .25 and .5 m on a pillar shadow. Contact should stay sharper than the far end; a fully occluded center can remain dark. Increasing radius should widen the penumbra without changing the diffuse attenuation curve. For reflective materials, separately compare Specular Scale 0/1 and the highlight's extent. Zero Source Radius restores the original point shadow.
5. Toggle Character Key/Fill separately. They should light the player without brightening architecture. Key Cast Shadow should preserve the fixed-direction player silhouette while pillar shadows follow the omni.
6. Enable Environment Ambient Darkness and tune Character Darkness Reveal Spread. Directional light, IBL, the point omni and Character lights must all fade with the combined darkness. Scene lights can reveal separate islands. Spread remains a distance mask and can reveal global illumination across a wall; room-aware visibility is separate future work.

Automatic occluder fading, wall-aware player visibility, AO, time-of-day/volume blending and monster/item rig classification remain separate future slices.
